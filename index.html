The short version

What we know.
What is not set.

The project is real. Many on-the-ground details are still in development.

ANNOUNCED

SpaceX plans a Louisiana site.

SpaceX and the state announced Starbase Louisiana near Pecan Island. Construction is targeted to begin by the end of 2027, with a first launch as soon as 2029.

IN DEVELOPMENT

Roads, a bridge, and a wharf are in review.

A coastal-use application describes access roads, a bridge, wharves, work areas, and dredging. These are not final permits or final plans.

NOT SET

Many local details remain open.

There is no final public road route, private-parcel buy list, launch schedule, utility plan, noise map, or answer for how any one property may be affected.

A large tract does not mean every acre is a building site.

The former Exxon property sold to SpaceX is about 125,000 acres. SpaceX says it will not develop the full footprint and plans to preserve wetlands and wildlife habitat. Public reporting also describes a smaller, roughly 40,000-acre coastal development area.

How this guide stays current

We are monitoring public updates from SpaceX, Louisiana Economic Development, DOTD, DNR/OCM, and other permitting agencies. When public documents change the picture, this website and handout can be updated.

Why this will not be Brownsville

Pecan Island is not a
repeat of Brownsville.

Craig’s experience gives us context. It does not create a blueprint. The land, nearby services, physical setting, and project stage are different.

Land scale — not an ownership ratio

Louisiana begins with a much larger known tract.

125,000 acres of former Exxon property were sold to SpaceX in Louisiana. SpaceX says it will not develop the entire tract.

A complete, current total for all SpaceX-owned land around Brownsville is not public. Federal 2026 exchange documents identify 683 acres of SpaceX-owned Texas property in one exchange—not the company’s full Texas footprint. A simple ownership ratio would mislead.

Community & service base

Brownsville has a larger nearby base.

Brownsville is a city of about 185,000 people with about 350 miles of paved streets. South Padre Island and Matamoros are minutes away, with more lodging, restaurants, stores, labor, and visitor activity already in the region.

Pecan Island is a chenier-and-marsh community about 10 miles inland from the Gulf. Its coastal setting, smaller service base, and limited access create a different starting point.

What’s the same / what’s different

The launch-rate gap is the clearest contrast.

What is the same
Both are Gulf Coast Starship sites. Both need heavy construction, workers, traffic management, power, water, coastal protection, and launch access over open water.
What is different
Louisiana starts with more documented acreage, more marsh, a smaller nearby service base, and a company that already has years of Texas Starship lessons.
Launch rate: now vs. goal
Texas launched 3 Starships from May 22–Sept. 28, 2026—about 1 every 6 weeks. Louisiana is planned for 10 pads and official capacity of thousands annually. Musk has separately stated a long-range aspiration of 30+ flights a day; that is not a permit or schedule.

Useful context: the FAA’s Texas environmental review covers up to 25 annual Starship/Super Heavy launches. That is a reviewed ceiling—not Texas’s current actual cadence and not a Louisiana approval.

The practical takeaway

Brownsville shows the kinds of community change a major spaceport can bring. Pecan Island’s larger tract, more marsh, smaller local service base, and newer project plan mean the details may unfold very differently here.

Workforce, housing & timing

A big project can change
daily life fast.

More workers can mean more traffic, more demand for places to stay, and more need for food, fuel, and services. The biggest unanswered question is when that demand would arrive.

Officially announced

3,000 direct jobs over 10 years

SpaceX and Louisiana say the project will create more than 3,000 direct jobs. SpaceX says it plans to hire heavily from nearby communities. The state also says the full site is expected to include employee housing.

What this means: many jobs may go to people who already live and work in Louisiana. We still do not know how many workers would need to stay near Pecan Island during construction.

Community scenario — not confirmed

30,000–35,000 workers at peak construction

Leaders and local sources have discussed this possible peak. We could not find an official SpaceX, state, or permit document that confirms that number, the peak year, or how long it would last.

Why it matters: if a large outside workforce arrives before housing catches up, rentals, RV sites, food, fuel, and local roads could feel pressure.

Texas example

Worker housing can take different forms.

Texas Starbase includes Airstream trailers, tiny homes, and houses. Louisiana employee housing is announced, but its location, count, timing, and design are not public yet.

What to watch

Timing will decide the pressure.

Official sources say construction is expected to begin in 2027, with a first launch targeted as soon as 2029. There is no official public peak-workforce schedule, bus plan, housing count, or rent forecast.

Large Cameron Parish projects show that worker transportation can be organized at scale. That does not mean SpaceX has chosen a bus plan for Pecan Island.

What we are monitoring

Public information about worker transportation, employee housing, traffic management, local services, and construction timing will help show when the largest pressure could arrive.

Land near the project

Private land:
what we know today.

There is no public private-parcel buy list. Land may matter to the project in different ways, but no owner should assume a specific need or offer.

Organizer-provided map

The former Exxon property

Green outlines show the organizer-provided former Exxon property map. It is a reference only—not an official SpaceX parcel map or a map of private land it plans to acquire.

Confirmed land transaction

The former Exxon property is already part of the project story.

Public reporting says Exxon donated a broader 125,000-acre property to Louisiana as part of a coastal-lawsuit settlement. The state then sold the property to SpaceX for $100 million.

Not publicly identified

No private-parcel need has been announced.

We did not find a public SpaceX map, offer list, or agency document naming privately owned parcels it wants to buy, lease, or use.

What people are saying about Highway 82 and Front Ridge Road

We have heard that privately owned land south of Highway 82, near Front Ridge Road, could be more important for project needs. We have also heard that land on or north of Highway 82 might still be of interest, but may be less critical. We could not verify either claim with SpaceX, DOTD, or a permitting agency. Treat it as local word-of-mouth—not a SpaceX plan.

Article one

SpaceX Is Coming to Louisiana. What Could It Mean for Pecan Island?

The announcement matters. But there is a big difference between an announcement, an application, and an approved plan.

Read the simple version ↗

The project is real. The final plan is not here yet.

SpaceX and Louisiana have announced a proposed Starbase Louisiana campus. The plan talks about launch work, power, shipping, and more. But it does not give us every final answer today.

Big numbers need care.

The state talks about a major investment and thousands of jobs over time. Those are goals. They are not promises about what will happen to one house, one camp, or one business.

Article two

Pecan Island Before Change Arrives

Brownsville can teach us about real change. It cannot tell us exactly what will happen here.

Read the simple version ↗

Brownsville has useful lessons.

SpaceX activity there changed traffic, access, noise, business activity, and local expectations. That gives Pecan Island a nearby example of a community experiencing a major spaceport.

Pecan Island is different.

Our roads, marshes, camps, homes, businesses, and storm risks are not the same as Texas. A new Louisiana project must be understood through Louisiana maps, Louisiana rules, and public information as it develops.

The goal

Stay informed. Stay connected. The community comes first.
